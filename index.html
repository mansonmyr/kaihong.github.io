Intro

I am a research-oriented biomedical science graduate with interdisciplinary training spanning medical biotechnology, health research ethics, and translational biomedical research. I hold a Master of Health Research Ethics from the University of Malaya, completed under a collaborative program with the Johns Hopkins Berman Institute of Bioethics and fully funded by the Fogarty International Center (NIH). My academic background is complemented by a dual-degree Bachelor of Science (Hons) in Medical Biotechnology from Lancaster University and Sunway University.

Previously, I work as a full-time Research Assistant at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou Campus). My research focuses on protein localization and kinase-regulated microtubule nucleation using advanced imaging platforms including confocal and STED microscopy. I am also involved in developing automated image-based quantification pipelines using object-detection machine learning, alongside manuscript preparation and review. My research experience extends across biomedical, clinical, and health systems research. I have contributed to systematic and umbrella reviews on knee osteoarthritis interventions, participated in mHealth development for HIV prevention in collaboration with Yale University and the University of Connecticut, and engaged in qualitative research involving community advisory boards. I have also published peer-reviewed articles in ISI- and SCOPUS-indexed journals through collaborative projects at the University of Malaya. In addition, I have industry experience in clinical research operations as a Trial Master File Specialist at Syneos Health, where I worked under GCP-compliant environments using Veeva Vault. My undergraduate research involved bioinformatic analysis of G-quadruplex structures in HIV-1 genomes for antiviral development.

Overall, my academic trajectory reflects a strong commitment to rigorous biomedical research, ethical clinical practice, and the integration of quantitative, experimental, and ethical perspectives to address complex biomedical and translational challenges.
